The problem

Writing a document is easy.
Knowing who else read it is hard.

Most office software is chatty. In the background, it is busy —

  • checking for updates
  • reporting crashes
  • syncing your settings
  • loading whatever a document asks it to

If you handle material that other people would like to read, that is a problem.

Anleo makes no network requests.
Not fewer — none.

One application, not four downloads

Four apps.
One window.

There is no account, no subscription, no cloud and nothing to sign up for. Your documents are files on your Mac and they stay there.

App 01 — Word processor

Anleo Docs

The formatting you actually use: styles, tables, lists, find and replace, page breaks, a table of contents, special characters, and system spellcheck with right-click suggestions.

  • Images you can genuinely move. Drag a picture anywhere and text flows around it — left wrap, right wrap or inline — without the layout collapsing. It exports as a real Word square-wrap anchor, so it survives the round trip.
  • Install your own fonts by dropping a .ttf or .otf into the app. No system installation, no admin password.

Anleo Slides

Themes, layouts, 15 shape types with gradient fills and arrowheads, align and distribute, transitions and slide numbers.

  • A presenter view that earns its name. Speaker notes and a timer on your screen while the deck plays on theirs.

App 04 — Forms & surveys

Anleo Forms

Eleven question types, sections, help text, required fields, themes, quiz marking and conditional branching — with a live preview of exactly what the person filling it in will see.

  • No link to publish. No account for anyone. Google Forms needs a server, so every answer lands in someone else's database. Anleo exports one self-contained HTML file. Send it however you like; they open it in any browser, offline, and fill it in.
  • Answers come back as a file or a pasted code. You import them and they aggregate, with per-question summaries and charts. Nobody in the middle ever sees them — the exported page carries a policy forbidding it from contacting anything, so a respondent can check that before typing a word.
  • Quizzes mark themselves, in the respondent's own browser, with no network. Responses export to CSV or go straight into a spreadsheet in Anleo Sheets.

Not a cheaper Office

Things Word and
Google Docs don't do.

01

Live links between the four apps

Copy a range in Sheets, paste it into a document or a slide, and it stays connected. Change the spreadsheet, hit refresh, and the table and its totals update wherever you pasted them. No cloud involved — it is all local.

02

Share as a web page

Turn any document into a single .html file that anyone can open by double-clicking, on Mac, Windows or a phone. Spreadsheets stay live and recalculate for the reader, decks play as a slideshow, checklists stay tickable.

03

A command palette

⌘K searches and runs every command in the app. ⌘/ lists them all with their shortcuts. No hunting through ribbons.

04

Fonts without an admin password

Drop a .ttf or .otf into the app and use it. Nothing is installed system-wide, nothing asks you to authenticate, nothing leaks to a foundry.

05

AI that is off by default

Rewrite, summarise, change tone, generate a formula from a description, or draft a deck outline. There is no bundled key. Add your own OpenRouter key and exactly one host opens in the gate; remove it and the app goes back to reaching nothing.

06

It costs nothing because it costs nothing to run

There is no server anywhere. No seat to meter, no storage to bill, no account to maintain. That is the whole business model, and it is why the price can be zero without an asterisk.

Why it's private

Enforced, rather
than intended.

"We don't collect your data" is a promise. This is a filter that cancels the request.

Layer one

A deny-by-default network gate

Every request that is not a local file is cancelled in the main process — which sits below the renderer. It holds even if the user interface is compromised.

Layer two

A strict Content-Security-Policy

A second, independent barrier. Two locks that fail in different ways are worth more than one lock that fails in one.

The tracking-pixel problem

A document you open cannot tell anyone you opened it

The oldest trick against a reporter is a one-pixel image hosted on the sender's server: open the file and they learn your IP address and the moment you read it. Word does this by default. Anleo strips remote images, scripts, iframes, remote stylesheets and javascript: links out of every file it imports — and tells you what it removed.

Install

macOS will block it
on first launch.

Builds are deliberately unsigned. Signing would stamp a real person's legal name and Apple Team ID into every binary, which is the wrong trade for this particular app. The cost is one extra step, once.

"Apple could not verify 'Anleo Office' is free of malware."

That message means the app has not been through Apple's paid notarization service. It is not a claim that anything was found.

On macOS 15 Sequoia and later — right-click → Open no longer works, Apple removed it:

  1. Drag Anleo Office to Applications and double-click it once. Click Done on the warning.
  2. Open the Apple menu → System Settings → Privacy & Security.
  3. Scroll to Security. You'll see "Anleo Office was blocked to protect your Mac." Click Open Anyway and authenticate.
  4. Double-click the app again and click Open. You only do this once.

On macOS 14 Sonoma and earlier: right-click the app → Open → Open.

Either version, one command
$ xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine "/Applications/Anleo Office.app"
